     4 +WHY THIS EXISTS. A rule file is written once and then assumed to work. Nothing

     6 +rule every session follows: the file is still there, and nothing turns red. That
     7 +is a DEAD RULE, and the only way to tell one from a live rule is to count.
     8 +
     9 +WHAT IT MEASURES, AND WHAT IT CANNOT. A small closed set of rule signatures that
    10 +are mechanically detectable in a transcript. That set is not the rule corpus and
    11 +it never will be: most rules are about judgement, and judgement leaves no
    12 +regular expression behind. So the number this produces is a floor on violations,
    13 +never a compliance score, and a rule with zero hits may be perfectly observed or
    14 +may simply have no detector. `conventions/dead-rules-audit.md` carries the
    15 +limits in full; read it before quoting a number from here.
    16 +
    17 +The four detectors, each named for the shape it finds rather than for the rule
    18 +it belongs to. That is deliberate — the shapes are generic, and the private rule
    19 +corpus that motivates them stays private:
    20 +
    21 +  compound-inspection   a shell command chaining three or more READ-ONLY
    22 +                        segments with `&&` or `;`. Each segment may be
    23 +                        permitted on its own and the chain still prompts,
    24 +                        because a compound command is scored as a whole.

    31 +                        never match a prefix allowlist.
