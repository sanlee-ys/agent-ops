     4 +THE PROBLEM. A loop that keeps or reverts a change based on one benchmark

     6 +no spread to compare against, so "the candidate scored better" and "the
     7 +candidate scored better by chance" look identical from inside a single
     8 +iteration. `conventions/loop-safety.md` names this: a loop re-earns the right
     9 +to merge through mechanical rails, and a metric-driven keep/revert call is one

    11 +
    12 +THE RULE, adapted from davebcn87/pi-autoresearch (MIT License, Copyright (c)
    13 +2026 Tobi Lutke, David Cortes), README section "Confidence Scoring Rule for
    14 +Benchmark-Driven Loops":
    15 +
    16 +    confidence = |best_improvement| / MAD(prior_runs)
    17 +
    18 +where MAD is the median absolute deviation of the prior runs from their own
    19 +median — a noise floor built from repeated measurements of the unchanged
    20 +baseline. At 2.0 or above the gain is likely real ("keep"); below 1.0 it is
    21 +within the noise floor ("noise"); in between the run count cannot yet tell
    22 +("inconclusive"). The source project requires three or more prior runs before
    23 +it scores at all, and this module keeps that floor.
    24 +

    26 +distribution (repeated measurements of the metric before the change).
    27 +`candidate` is the one new measurement after the change. `best_improvement` is
    28 +the candidate's distance from `median(prior_runs)`, signed so that a positive
    29 +value always means "better", per the `direction` flag:
    30 +
    31 +    lower-is-better:  best_improvement = median(prior_runs) - candidate
    32 +    higher-is-better: best_improvement = candidate - median(prior_runs)
